02 — The Paradox

Strong regional assets.
Misaligned opportunity.

—
Pilot: graduate outflow rate

In our pilot market (Korea's Daejeon–Chungcheong region), a majority of university graduates leave for capital cities early in their careers — despite strong R&D and university density. The region exports talent instead of retaining verified work history.

—
Applicants per role (non-STEM cohorts)

This is not a major-specific issue — it is a structural verification gap. Employers cannot assess proof-of-work; candidates cannot prove capability without capital-city networks.

Youth want pathways to build capability; regional employers need deployable talent. Job boards optimize for credentials, not verified outcomes. The missing layer is trusted proof-of-work infrastructure — which we call LER (Learning & Employment Records).

03 — The Shift

In the AI economy,
proof beats pedigree.

Routine admin tasks,  Basic research,  Data entry are being automated.
What employers need now:  Non-routine problem solving,  Verified work records,  LER portfolios.
[ 01 ]
Junior tasks are automating

Generative AI is compressing entry-level knowledge work. The traditional apprentice model for building experience is breaking — increasing demand for verifiable, project-based proof.

[ 02 ]
Credentials → proof

Degrees alone no longer de-risk hiring. Employers increasingly require evidence of outcomes — not self-reported resumes.

[ 03 ]
Regional double bind

Without local proof-of-work rails, regional talent competes globally with no portable evidence — a pattern repeated in secondary cities worldwide.
